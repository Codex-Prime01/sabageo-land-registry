Field data kit

Collecting real plot corners in the field.

Everything a survey team needs to measure a plot and bring the corners back to SABAGeo: a field sheet to print, a CSV template to fill in, and the one question that must be answered before you start.

First: which datum?

The same numbers mean different places on the ground depending on the datum they were measured in. In Nigeria that is usually either WGS84 (GPS, phones, most modern receivers) or Minna (older survey plans and many government records).

Near Biu, the same coordinates read as Minna instead of WGS84 land about 70 metres apart. That is enough to put a plot on the wrong street, so always write the datum down.

If the coordinates are…Choose this type in SABAGeo
From a phone or handheld GPS, in degreesLat / Long (WGS84)
From a GNSS receiver set to WGS84 UTMUTM 31N, 32N or 33N (WGS84)
From an old survey plan, in degreesLat / Long (Minna)
From a plan in Minna UTMUTM 31N, 32N or 33N (Minna)
From a plan in Nigerian beltsNigeria West, Mid or East Belt (Minna)

Which UTM zone? West of 6°E is zone 31N (Lagos, Ibadan, Ilorin, Benin City). From 6°E to 12°E is 32N (Abuja, Kano, Gombe, Owerri, Port Harcourt). East of 12°E is 33N: Biu and Maiduguri.

Honest note: SABAGeo converts Minna to WGS84 with the standard Nigeria shift. That is good to a few metres, not centimetres. For legal-grade work, use coordinates already in WGS84 from a GNSS receiver, or a local transformation from a licensed surveyor.

The CSV template

One row per corner, in order around the plot. Fill in either easting and northing (metres) or latitude and longitude (degrees). Put the datum and the zone or belt on the first row; SABAGeo reads them and picks the right coordinate type for you.

In the field

  1. Find every beacon. Number them going round the plot, one way only (clockwise is fine).
  2. Measure each beacon and write it on the field sheet straight away, with the datum and zone.
  3. Take a photo at each beacon with the phone's location turned on. SABAGeo checks the photo's GPS tag against the plot.
  4. Check the side lengths on the ground with a tape. They should match the coordinates to within a few centimetres.
  5. Note anything odd: a missing beacon, a fence off the line, a building on the boundary.

Sending it in

  • Pilot plots (NAUB): the administrator opens the map, unlocks with the admin key, chooses the NAUB region, uploads the CSV and registers it. It fills the next pilot slot.
  • Clients: log in on Register New Plot, upload the CSV, check for overlap, register, then add the beacon photos.
  • Keep the paper field sheet. It is the record of who measured what, and when.
