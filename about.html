About us

Land records you can check, built from real survey data.

SABAGeo Technology Limited is a student-led innovation from the Nigerian Army University Biu (NAUB) in Borno State. We work in PropTech: we take accurate geospatial survey data and lock it into a tamper-evident record, so land boundaries can be checked instead of argued over.

What we want to change

Land administration is still weighed down by manual errors, corruption and court cases that drag on for years. Double allocation, overlapping boundaries and encroachment are common, and paper plans are hard to compare by eye. SABAGeo is built to make the process more transparent, more secure and easier to verify.

How a record is protected

Every new plot is checked against every registered plot, so the same land cannot be registered twice. Once a boundary is verified, the record is sealed with a cryptographic fingerprint, and that fingerprint is posted on the Polygon blockchain network. After that, any later change to the record can be detected.

What it does

  • Checks a new plot against all registered land and answers with a green tick (0 overlap) or a red X (overlap).
  • Gives each registered plot a unique title code.
  • Stores a geotagged beacon photo and checks how far from the plot it was taken.
  • Keeps a tamper-evident ledger of every registration.
  • Posts the ledger's latest fingerprint on the Polygon test network, as a public timestamp.

Pilot area and coverage

The pilot area is the NAUB staff quarters and the Biu-Gombe Road layout expansion. Five pilot slots are reserved for real field measurements, and they always come first.

The platform also holds synthetic test data for Ikeja (Lagos), Abuja, Gombe, Kano, Maiduguri, the Borno-Biu Emirate, Ibadan, Port Harcourt, Benin City, Owerri and Ilorin. It is there to simulate the system, and it is labelled that way.

Where it stands

  • It is a working prototype, not a legal land registry.
  • The blockchain part runs on a test network, so those records are for demonstration only.
  • A ledger fingerprint shows a record has not changed since it was written. It does not prove the survey measurement was correct. That depends on good fieldwork.
